How to lock your crypto so you can't sell it

If you've ever sworn you'd hold for years and then dumped the moment the chart went red, the problem isn't your strategy — it's that the sell button is always right there. Here's how to take it away from yourself.

Most people don't lose money in crypto because they picked the wrong coin. They lose it in the ten seconds where fear takes over and they sell at the bottom — the exact thing they promised themselves they'd never do. Willpower is the first thing to break when the market drops, so relying on it doesn't work.

The fix isn't more discipline. It's removing the option. Below are the real ways to lock your crypto, from weakest to strongest, and a plain step-by-step for the method that actually makes early selling impossible.

What locking your crypto actually means

Locking means putting your crypto somewhere you can't easily reach it for a set period. Some methods are just friction — they slow you down. Others are absolute — they make selling genuinely impossible until a date you choose.

The difference matters, because in a real panic, friction isn't enough. If a method can be undone in the heat of the moment, sooner or later it will be.

How a time-lock vault makes selling impossible

A time-lock vault is a smart contract. You deposit your ETH or tokens, pick an unlock date, and confirm. From that moment the funds are held by code on the blockchain — and that code contains no function to release them early. There's no emergency withdraw, no override, no support line that can let you out. When your date arrives, and only then, you withdraw.

Crucially, this is self-custody. The vault never hands your crypto to a company. It sits in a public contract that only your wallet can withdraw from, once the timer hits zero. Nobody — not the developer, not a stranger with the contract address — can move it early. You've locked it against the one person who tends to break the plan: you, at your worst moment.

Who should lock their crypto this way

Time-locking is for the money you've already decided to hold long term but keep selling early anyway. If you're a long-term believer who folds every dip, someone taking profit-target discipline seriously, or anyone who wants to force themselves to hold through a full cycle — this is built for you.

It's not for funds you might genuinely need soon. Lock what should sit; keep the rest liquid.

Frequently asked questions

How do I lock my crypto so I can't sell it?

Use a time-lock vault. You deposit your ETH or tokens into a smart contract, choose an unlock date, and the funds can't be withdrawn until that date passes. Because there's no early-withdrawal function, selling in a panic isn't possible while the lock is active.

Can I unlock my crypto early?

No. With a genuine time-lock like CryptoTimeLock, the contract contains no function to withdraw before the unlock date. Not you, not the developer, not anyone else can release the funds early. That's the entire point.

Is time-locking my crypto safe?

It's self-custody. Your crypto is held by a public smart contract on Ethereum, not by a company. Only your own wallet can withdraw it, and only after the unlock date — so there's no account for anyone to freeze and no central party holding your keys.

How long can I lock my crypto for?

You choose. Lock for a few weeks, a year, or as long as ten years. The unlock date is set when you create the lock and can't be shortened afterwards.

What does it cost?

CryptoTimeLock charges a one-time 0.5% fee when you create the lock. Withdrawing after the unlock date is free. You also pay standard Ethereum gas for each transaction.
